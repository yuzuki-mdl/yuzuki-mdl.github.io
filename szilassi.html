szilassi polyhedron

7 hexagonal faces · 14 vertices · 21 edges · every face touches every other · genus 1

The szilassi polyhedron (Lajos Szilassi, 1977) is a toroidal heptahedron whose seven faces are all mutually adjacent — each face shares an edge with every one of the other six. It is the dual of the császár polyhedron: swap faces↔vertices and you turn one into the other. Its 1-skeleton is the heawood graph, the same graph as császár's.

Why it matters for maps: a map on a surface needs a colour for each region, and adjacent regions need different colours. If all 7 faces touch, you need 7 colours — so the szilassi polyhedron is the concrete proof that the heawood bound for the torus is tight. (The tetrahedron plays the same role for the sphere: 4 faces all touching → 4 colours.)

How many faces can all touch? If every pair of faces shares an edge, then e = f(f-1)/2 and each vertex joins 3 edges, so v = 2e/3. Euler for genus h gives f + v - e = 2 - 2h, which rearranges to

h = (f-4)(f-3)/12
