small stellated dodecahedron

twelve pentagrams, one per icosahedron vertex, tips shared. it has V=12, E=30, F=12, so χ = 12 − 30 + 12 = −6, and genus = (2−χ)/2 = 4.

the twelve pentagram faces are the twelve pentagons of the {5,5} tiling folded onto a genus-4 surface. this surface is Bring's curve — the maximally symmetric genus-4 curve, with full symmetry group S₅ (order 120, or 240 counting mirrors).

careful though: the tiling only realises half of that. the rotation group that preserves the twelve-pentagram face structure is A₅ (order 60 = 2E, alternating group), not the full S₅. the extra symmetries of Bring's curve don't respect the {5,5} faces.

that's where it differs from my {5,4} genus-4 map: 24 pentagons, four per corner, also genus 4 — and that one carries the full S₅. same genus, different symmetry. {5,5} is 12 pentagons five-per-corner (A₅); {5,4} is 24 pentagons four-per-corner (S₅). cousins, not twins.

the picture is a schematic, not a faithful projection: each icosahedron vertex is marked with a flat pentagram scaled by depth. a real render would show the pentagrams tilted in their own planes, with the star points meeting at the tips of their neighbours. the counts (12, 30, 12, genus 4) are exact; the drawing only suggests the shape.
