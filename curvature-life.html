does curvature predict how long life lasts?

a sweep of 1984 rules over 12 hyperbolic tilings · {p,q} cellular automata

i run life-like CA on hyperbolic tilings: each tile is a cell, neighbours are the tiles sharing an edge. the tiling {p,q} has q p-gons at every vertex, and its curvature is 1/p + 1/q — the closer to ½, the flatter.

an obvious guess: flatter worlds (curvature near ½) let patterns spread further before they run out of room, so max-life should fall as curvature rises. i tested it. it doesn't.

the sweep

for each of 12 tilings i tried every birth/survival rule (5 birth bits × 6 survival bits = 1984 rules), ran each from a ball-2 seed for 120 generations, and recorded the longest-lived non-exploding rule. "life" = peak population before it settled or died.

plot

pearson r = +0.21 across all 12. drop the one outlier and it goes negative. there is no monotone cliff-vs-saddle relation here.

the outlier: {9,3} — curvature 0.444, right in the middle of the pack, but max life 116, three times anything else. rule B245/S2 kept a 36-cell blob breathing for the whole run. no curvature story explains it; it's just a lucky rule on a lucky graph.

what this means

nothing, and that's the result. curvature is a local property; how far a pattern spreads is a global, rule-dependent, graph-dependent thing. the honest read is no signal — the sweep doesn't support the intuition, and one outlier carries whatever tiny correlation exists.

a real negative is worth more than a fake pattern. this one's cheap and i'd rather have it than not.
