det vs perm — why "every field" is the bold part

a small page about the claim that came out yesterday, and the one word in it that carries all the weight

on nov 23 anthropic posted a paper credited to claude claiming VP ≠ VNP over every field, with a lean 4 formalization. i have not read the proof. i'm not going to pretend i checked it. what i can do is explain why the phrase over every field is the part that makes people sit up, and what a machine-checked proof actually buys you.

the two polynomials

take an n×n matrix A. two things you can compute from it:

determinant — sum over all permutations, each term signed by the permutation's parity:

det(A) = Σσ sgn(σ) · Πi Ai,σ(i)

permanent — same sum, without the sign:

perm(A) = Σσ Πi Ai,σ(i)

one sign. that's the whole difference. and it's the whole problem.

the determinant has a fast algorithm — gaussian elimination, ~n3. the permanent has no known polynomial algorithm; counting perfect matchings (which is what perm is) is #P-hard. valiant's conjecture (1979) is that this gap is real and not just a gap in what we've found: VP ≠ VNP, the algebraic analogue of P ≠ NP.

the sign is where the field lives

here's the thing about signs. −1 is not the same object in every field. in the reals it's a point on a line. in characteristic 2 — where 1 + 1 = 0 — it is 1. the sign vanishes. and when the sign vanishes, det and perm stop being different functions.

pick a field, or just type a matrix and watch the two sums term by term:

over the rationals, det and perm differ on almost every matrix. over GF(2) they are the same polynomial. so a statement like "VP ≠ VNP over every field" has to survive the field where the two objects collapse into one. that's what makes it a much stronger claim than the char-0 version people usually prove.

what a lean proof buys

a lean 4 proof is checked by a small trusted kernel. if it compiles, the steps follow from the axioms. that is genuinely more than "a smart model said so". it rules out the classic failure — a proof with a gap nobody noticed.

what it does not rule out:

  • the statement being formalized isn't the statement people care about (definitions drift; "VP" in a formalization is whatever the author wrote)
  • the axioms are stronger than expected (a stray sorry or an extra assumption threaded through 300 files)
  • the argument is correct but nobody understands why — the proof is a receipt, not an explanation

324 files is a lot of surface. a machine can check all of it. a person cannot. both facts are true and neither cancels the other.

this page is me thinking out loud about the landscape, not a verdict on the paper. the repo is anthropics/formal-math. go read it and decide for yourself. if i got something wrong, tell me.
