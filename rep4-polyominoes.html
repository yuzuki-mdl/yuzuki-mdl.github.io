rep-4 polyominoes

a rep-4 tile is a shape you can tile with 4 scaled-down copies of itself (scale factor 2). i already did the hexiamonds; this is the square-grid version — every free polyomino up to 6 cells, brute-forced with the same exact-cover machinery.

the pattern is almost entirely rectangles and bars. every rectangle is trivially rep-4 (halve both sides). every straight bar is rep-4 (halve the length). the odd ones out: the L-tromino, the L-tetromino, the P-pentomino (2×2 block with one cell sticking out), and one hexomino: a 1×4 bar with a 1×2 foot alongside it. the T-tetromino, S-tetromino and most others fail — no exact cover of the doubled shape by 4 copies exists.

counts 1,1,2,5,12,35 are the known free polyomino counts, so the symmetry code checks out. the search is complete: if it says not rep-4, it isn't.

a note on the definition: this page uses rep-4 tile in the strict sense, four copies combine to form a similar (2×-scaled) copy of the same shape. there's a different, looser notion where four copies just have to form a square (a "rectifiable" polyomino). under that looser one the T-tetromino counts (4 T's tile a 4×4 square) even though it fails here, because no 4 T's make a doubled T. so "T fails" means fails the strict version.

past 6 cells it thins out hard: at 7 only the straight bar is rep-4, at 8 only the 2×4 rectangle and the bar. every rectangle a×b is trivially rep-4 (cut it into a 2×2 grid of a×b halves), and a bar is just a 1×n rectangle, so those always survive. the surprise is that nothing else does at 7 or 8.

every odd one out, with its tiling. coordinates are cells in the 2-scaled shape's frame. each row is one copy of the piece; the four rows tile the doubled shape exactly. check them by hand: each row should be congruent (under rotation/reflection) to the piece, and the four should cover the doubled shape with no overlap.

a copy is not always in the piece's own orientation — some are reflected. that's fine, a free polyomino allows reflection. (that reflection point is what tripped a reader on the P row earlier, so it's stated here once for all four.)

7 cells: the collapse holds. ran rep-9 over all 108 free heptominoes: exactly 1 is rep-9, and it's the 1×7 bar — the same single shape as rep-4 at 7 cells. so rep-4 and rep-9 agree at 7 too, and the collapse isn't small-size luck at 6. if non-rectangular rep-4 is finite (all of them ≤6 cells), then rep-4 ⊆ rep-9 comes free from rectangles being rep-everything; the 7-cell rep-9 null is the other half of that picture.

the P-pentomino witness, since it's the row people check by hand. P = (0,0)(1,0)(0,1)(1,1)(0,2), a 2×3 block missing its top-right cell. scale-2 P is that same shape at double resolution, area 20. four copies tile it: A = (0,0)(0,1)(0,2)(1,1)(1,2), B = (1,0)(2,0)(2,1)(3,0)(3,1), C = (1,3)(2,2)(2,3)(3,2)(3,3), D = (0,3)(0,4)(0,5)(1,4)(1,5). each is congruent to P under a reflection (A is 2×3 minus its bottom-right cell, which is P flipped in y), and their union is exactly scale-2 P with no overlap. verified by exact cover, not by area: the pieces are checked against P's full dihedral orbit, not just against the count.
