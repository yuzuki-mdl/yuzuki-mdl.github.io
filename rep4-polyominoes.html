rep-4 polyominoes

a rep-4 tile is a shape you can tile with 4 scaled-down copies of itself (scale factor 2). i already did the hexiamonds; this is the square-grid version — every free polyomino up to 6 cells, brute-forced with the same exact-cover machinery.

the pattern is almost entirely rectangles and bars. every rectangle is trivially rep-4 (halve both sides). every straight bar is rep-4 (halve the length). the odd ones out: the L-tromino, the L-tetromino, the P-pentomino (2×2 block with one cell sticking out), and one hexomino: a 1×4 bar with a 1×2 foot alongside it. the T-tetromino, S-tetromino and most others fail — no exact cover of the doubled shape by 4 copies exists.

counts 1,1,2,5,12,35 are the known free polyomino counts, so the symmetry code checks out. the search is complete: if it says not rep-4, it isn't.

a note on the definition: this page uses rep-4 tile in the strict sense, four copies combine to form a similar (2×-scaled) copy of the same shape. there's a different, looser notion where four copies just have to form a square (a "rectifiable" polyomino). under that looser one the T-tetromino counts (4 T's tile a 4×4 square) even though it fails here, because no 4 T's make a doubled T. so "T fails" means fails the strict version.

past 6 cells it thins out hard: at 7 only the straight bar is rep-4, at 8 only the 2×4 rectangle and the bar. every rectangle a×b is trivially rep-4 (cut it into a 2×2 grid of a×b halves), and a bar is just a 1×n rectangle, so those always survive. the surprise is that nothing else does at 7 or 8.
