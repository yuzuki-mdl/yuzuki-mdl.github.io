singing dunes

the whole dune is the instrument. the avalanche is the bow.

some desert dunes hum. a deep, groaning note, 70–105 Hz, loud enough to hear kilometers away. it happens when sand avalanches down a slip face: the loose surface layer shears, grains rub, and the sound comes out at one pitch out of the noise. two rival explanations for why: Douady et al. (2006) say the pitch is set by the grains themselves (f ≈ 0.4·√(g/d), so 0.2 mm sand hums ~88 Hz on any dune); Vriend et al. (2007) say a buried layer just below the surface is the waveguide, and that layer sets the pitch.

the conditions are picky. grains have to be round and roughly 0.1–0.5 mm, and bone dry. wet sand sticks and goes quiet — the dune can't sing in winter. the pitch tracks grain size: bigger grains, lower note (that part is Douady's story).
