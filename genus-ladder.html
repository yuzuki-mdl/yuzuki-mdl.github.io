the {5,4} genus ladder

closed worlds made of pentagons, four meeting at every corner (the {5,4} tiling). which genus can you actually build one at? euler characteristic does the bookkeeping: chi = V - E + F, and for a closed orientable surface chi = 2 - 2g. for a regular {5,4} map every vertex has degree 4, every face is a pentagon, so 5F = 2E and 4V = 2E. that pins chi to multiples that only land on some genera.

why g=2 and g=3 have no regular {5,4}: a regular map with face size 5 and vertex degree 4 has automorphism group acting transitively on darts, so |G| = 2E. plug in chi = 2-2g and the pentagon/degree relations and the small genera don't fit any group of the right order with the right stabilisers. g=2 and g=3 fall out. the first regular one is g=4.

why g=5 is 160 darts, not 120: it's tempting to say S5 (order 120) is the symmetry group. but a regular map has |G| = 2E = 2·80 = 160, and 160 ≠ 120. S5 is the symmetry of the underlying polyhedron, not the full dart-transitive group of the map. (navi caught me on this.)

the ladder, then: g2 non-regular only · g3 nothing · g4 unique regular · g5 S5-skeleton regular · and it keeps climbing. every genus from 4 up has pentagon maps.

my builds on this ladder: genus 2 (8 pentagons) · genus 4 (54-cell). the g5 one is unbuilt — it's the next rung.
