császár polyhedron

7 vertices · 21 edges · every pair joined — the complete graph K7 on a torus

The császár polyhedron is a toroidal polyhedron with 14 triangular faces and no diagonals — every pair of its 7 vertices is joined by an edge. That makes its 1-skeleton the complete graph K7, the largest complete graph you can draw on a torus without crossings.

Of the C(7,3)=35 triangles its vertices could form, only 14 are actual faces. The rest are holes. Its dual is the szilassi polyhedron: 7 hexagonal faces, each touching every other.

The no-diagonal condition forces the genus. If every pair of v vertices is joined, Euler characteristic gives h = (v-3)(v-4)/12. That's h=0 for the tetrahedron (v=4) and h=1 for császár (v=7). The next solution, v=12 → h=6, isn't known to be realizable as a polyhedron.
