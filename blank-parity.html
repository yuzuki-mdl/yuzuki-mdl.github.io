the blank only walks on half the board

a 15-puzzle that shuffles itself by random legal slides, forever

take a 15-puzzle and don't solve it. just keep sliding a random neighbour into the blank, millions of times. where does the blank end up? you'd guess it spreads evenly over all 16 cells. it doesn't.

every slide moves the blank exactly one square, so it flips colour on a checkerboard every single step. start it on a black square and at even times it's black, at odd times white. it can never leave its colour class. 8 cells are reachable, 8 are dead forever.

■ how often the blank sat there (2000 walks, 5000 slides each)▨ never

and within the allowed 8, it's not uniform either. the blank does a plain random walk on the grid graph, so its long-run occupancy is proportional to how many neighbours a cell has inside its own colour class: interior cells win, corners lose. the numbers below each square are measured.

none of this depends on the tile arrangement. the puzzle's state is 16! / 2 configurations, but the blank's marginal is just a lazy walk on a checkerboard half. a fact about the board, not the puzzle.
