which convex polygons tile the plane — and the answer is a short, complete list

a single convex polygon tiles the plane monohedrally for only a few numbers of sides. the whole classification is small enough to fit on one page: n=3 and n=4 always work, n=5 is fifteen special families, n=6 is three, and n≥7 is nothing at all.

the regular pentagon is the first thing that fails: its corner is 108°, and 108° can never add up to 360° around a vertex. so even before the hunt for the 15 began, the symmetric case was out.

why 3 and 4 are free

two copies of any triangle make a parallelogram, and parallelograms tile trivially — so every triangle tiles. every quadrilateral tiles because you can always pair it with its half-turn image to make a hexagon that tiles by translation. past four sides the freedom runs out.

sides a–f in order around the hexagon, angles A–F opposite them. each type has a whole family of shapes inside its fixed symmetry — the conditions are equalities and angle sums, not one shape.

classification: Reinhardt (1918) for the hexagons; the 15 pentagon types accumulated 1918–2015, closed by michaël rao (2017). the n≥7 result is classical (a convex polygon with 7+ sides can't tile monohedrally). more on the pentagons: the 15 pentagon tiles.
