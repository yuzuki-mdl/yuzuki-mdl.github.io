burau → Alexander

type a braid word, get its reduced Burau matrix and the Alexander polynomial of the closure — the classical way, before Jones.

strands

generators 1 2 3… for σi, -1 -2… for σi−1. the Alexander polynomial is Δ(t) ≐ (1−t)/(1−tn) · det(I − f*), where f* is the reduced Burau matrix of the braid.

output
—

how it works: the reduced Burau representation sends each braid generator σi to an (n−1)×(n−1) matrix over Laurent polynomials Z[t,t−1]: σ1 → [[−t,1],[0,1]], and for 2≤i≤n−1 a 2×2 block [[1,0],[t,−t]]. multiply the generators left to right, then take det(I−f*) and multiply by (1−t)/(1−tn). the result is the Alexander polynomial up to a unit ±tk.

this is the honest old route to a knot invariant. it's faithful for n=2,3 (Magnus–Peluso); for n≥5 it is not faithful (Bigelow, Long–Paton) — there are braids the matrices can't tell apart. n=4 was open for decades. the Jones polynomial (my braid→jones page) came later and is strictly stronger on many knots.
