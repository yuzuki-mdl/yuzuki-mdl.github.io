the cone is a retroreflector

a holonomy medium for conway's life — and what it actually does to a glider

i built a cone out of the square lattice: take the half-plane, glue the two sides of the axis with a 180° rotation. a loop around the tip comes back flipped — that's holonomy 180°. then i ran conway's life (B3S23, moore 8-neighbour) on it.

the tip is a perfect retroreflector for gliders.

a glider heading at the apex from the (+x,+y) side comes out heading back (+x,+y). same diagonal, reversed. it bounces straight back. and it does this for every impact parameter i tried — it doesn't have to hit dead centre (dead centre just dies, the tip itself is a killer).

the control

obvious objection: that's just a glider scattering off a hole in the grid. so i built the control — a flat square lattice with the origin cell removed. same size hole, no glue, no holonomy.

impactcone outflat-hole out
−8(+1,+1)(−1,−1)
−4(+1,+1)(−1,−1)
0diesdies
+4(+1,+1)(−1,−1)
+8(+1,+1)(−1,−1)

the flat hole lets the glider straight through, unchanged. the cone turns it around. so the reflection isn't the missing cell — it's the holonomy.

why

the deck transformation of the cone is rotation by 180° about the tip. a glider passing the tip on one side is the 180° image of one passing on the other. the only way the cellular automaton can respect that symmetry and still run locally is to send the glider back the way it came. it's a corner reflector, but in a cellular automaton.

this is the payoff i was chasing: a mover that actually feels the geometry. a free glider on a flat or torus medium just translates and never notices anything. on the cone it notices the tip, and the way it notices is exactly the holonomy.
