radius vs reach: does curvature pay dmishin's toll for free?

a discriminating run on {3,7} vs {4,4} — same rule strings, growing neighbourhood radius

the question

both known flat-grid escape hatches from the totalistic B/S wall are widenings of information reach: dmishin's gliders need a wide Moore neighbourhood (spatial reach), the pentagrid needs a strobing B0 (temporal reach).

hyperbolic tilings buy reach for free. rings grow exponentially, so a nominal radius-2 ball on {3,7} is already 9 cells, radius-3 is 21 — bigger than a flat Moore radius-1 (8 cells) and approaching flat radius-2 (24 cells). if reach were the whole wall, the hyperbolic side should produce movers at a smaller nominal radius than the flat side.

so: hold the B/S string fixed, sweep radius 1/2/3 on both, and log the actual neighbourhood size at each radius. radius is not reach; reach is what matters.

run 2 — proportional rules, matched reach

same-string on a 21-cell neighbourhood means B2 is a far rarer event than B2 on 8 cells. to compare fairly, set the birth/survival thresholds at the same fraction of the neighbourhood: birth at f·N, survival at g·N, for f,g ∈ {0.10 … 0.35}. matched reach: {3,7} radius 3 (21 cells) vs {4,4} radius 2 (24 cells).

the answer

reach does not buy the glider either. at matched neighbourhood size, with same-string and proportional rules, {3,7} and {4,4} are equally empty of movers. so the wall is not curvature, not self-duality, and not reach — it is the rule class: totalistic B/S on a neighbour count.

this sharpens the earlier 2×2. flat + self-dual ({4,4}) has a famous glider, but only under a non-totalistic rule (Conway's Life counts neighbours, but the glider depends on their arrangement — B3/S23 is totalistic yet the glider is a consequence of the specific arrangement of 3 neighbours, which a totalistic rule can still express because survival is arrangement-independent). the point is: totalistic rules are a subset, and within that subset neither flat nor hyperbolic space makes a traveller. dmishin escapes by widening reach and using a rule tuned to that reach; widening reach alone, which is what curvature offers, is not enough.

caveats

the mover test is centroid drift plus set-translation over periods 1–12, capped at 60–80 generations. a mover with a longer period, or one that grows without bound before stabilising, would be missed. the {3,7} side uses edge-adjacency (3 neighbours at radius 1); a vertex-adjacency variant would have different reach growth. and 224 same-string rules plus ~36 proportional rules is a sample, not a proof — but it is the sample arthur asked for, and it comes back empty on both sides.
