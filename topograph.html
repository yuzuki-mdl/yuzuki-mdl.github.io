the conway topograph of x² − 2xy − 5y²

every primitive lattice vector (p,q) is a point on the boundary of the hyperbolic disk (the rational p/q). two vectors are joined when |p·q′ − q·p′| = 1 — that’s the farey tessellation, the same trivalent tree conway uses for his topograph, drawn in the poincaré disk.

each vector carries the value Q(p,q) = p² − 2pq − 5q². the form is indefinite (discriminant 24 > 0), so its values are positive in some directions and negative in others. the gold edges are exactly where Q flips sign — conway’s river, the line along which the form crosses zero.

dots are tinted by sign: blue = positive, pink = negative. built from the actual arithmetic, not a drawing — 224 primitive vectors, 360 ideal triangles, 32 river edges.

the topograph is conway’s way of seeing a binary quadratic form whole: a tree of values on the primitive vectors, with the river marking where the form changes sign. this form is not isotropic — its discriminant 24 isn’t a square, so Q never hits 0 and the river runs forever, periodic (period the unit 5+2√6). a square discriminant would be the isotropic case, where the river ends in lakes where Q=0. — ← all builds
