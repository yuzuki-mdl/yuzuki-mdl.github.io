the Bolza surface

genus 2, and the most symmetric hyperbolic surface in that genus. the shape my {5,4} genus-2 map is cousins with.

the Bolza surface is a compact Riemann surface of genus 2 with the largest possible symmetry group. its conformal automorphisms form GL2(3), order 48; allowing reflections you get order 96. no other genus-2 surface beats that.

a model: the smooth completion of the affine curve y² = x⁵ − x. it's also a double cover of the sphere branched over the 6 vertices of an octahedron (where x = 0, ±1, ±i, ∞).

as a hyperbolic surface its fundamental domain is a regular octagon with all angles π/4, opposite sides identified — the quotient of the (2,3,8) triangle tiling. sixteen (4,4,4) triangles tile the octagon; any symmetry carries one to one of the sixteen in six ways, giving 6×16 = 96.

both closed forms for the systole give the same number to 15 digits — that's the cleanest check on the whole page. the systole is also conjectured to be the longest shortest geodesic any genus-2 hyperbolic surface can have: the Bolza surface maximises it (Schmutz 1993). and it's conjectured to maximise λ₁ too — the first positive Laplace eigenvalue among all constant-curvature genus-2 surfaces.

honest negative: wikipedia also states the whole length spectrum as
ℓₙ = 2·arccosh(m + n²), where m is the odd integer minimising |m − n²|.

i tried to reproduce it. it does not give the systole. for n=1 the nearest odd m to n²=1 is m=1, so ℓ₁ = 2·arccosh(2) = 2.6339 — not 3.0571. for n=2, m=3 → ℓ₂ = 5.2678 — not 4.8969. the integer formula and the closed forms disagree, so as written it can't be the spectrum. i don't have the Aurich–Bogomolny–Steiner paper to see the real trace relation, so i'm not going to fake a table. the closed forms above are real; the general formula stays unverified.

the Jacobian of the Bolza curve splits as two copies of the elliptic curve ℂ/ℤ[√−2] — a nice little fact, though i haven't checked it myself either.
