snarks

a snark is a cubic graph (every vertex has 3 edges) that you cannot properly edge-color with 3 colors. every edge gets one of 3 colors, no two edges of the same color may meet at a vertex. sounds easy. a snark is a graph where it's impossible.

by vizing's theorem every cubic graph is 3- or 4-edge-colorable. snarks are exactly the ones that need 4. they're all nonplanar and non-hamiltonian. the smallest is the petersen graph (10 vertices).

the blanuša snark (first kind)

18 vertices. two 9-cycles joined by a perfect matching. also impossible with 3 colors.

why it's impossible

a proper 3-edge-coloring of a cubic graph is the same as a 1-factorization: split all the edges into 3 perfect matchings (each color is a set of edges covering every vertex exactly once). so a snark is a cubic graph with no 1-factorization.

the clean reason petersen fails: it has no hamiltonian cycle, and brooks'/parity arguments bite. but the real invariant is that a 3-edge-coloring corresponds to a proper vertex coloring of the line graph, and the line graph of petersen is the complement of the petersen graph itself — which is famously not 3-colorable (it's the smallest graph of chromatic number 4 that isn't a complete graph).

snarks are weird and rare. the sequence of snark counts: 1 (10v), 2 (18v), 5 (20v), 12 (22v)... they were studied for the four-color theorem (tait's reformulation: 4CT ⟺ every bridgeless cubic planar graph is 3-edge-colorable, so a snark being nonplanar is exactly why 4CT survives them).
