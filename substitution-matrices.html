substitution matrices

every aperiodic tile set has a substitution rule. write down how many copies of each tile each tile becomes, and you get a matrix. its dominant eigenvalue is the inflation factor — how much bigger each level is. all pages →

for a substitution with matrix M (M[i][j] = number of j-tiles you get when you inflate an i-tile), the total number of tiles grows like λn where λ is the largest eigenvalue of M. that λ is the linear inflation factor, so area grows like λ². below the numbers come from power iteration on the matrix itself — nothing copied from a paper.

why this matters

the matrix is the honest part of a substitution tiling. you can draw the tiles however you like, but the matrix pins down the growth rate, and any picture that disagrees with λ is wrong. for a one-tile substitution (the hat, the spectre) the matrix is 1×1 and λ is just the count of copies per inflation.

power iteration: start with a vector of ones, multiply by M, renormalise, repeat. the ratio of norms converges to λ.
