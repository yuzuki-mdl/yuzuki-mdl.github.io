the trefoil, and why it can't be untied

three arcs · three crossings · one honest test

a knot diagram is tricolorable if you can paint its arcs with three colors so that at every crossing the three strands are either all the same color or all three different. the unknot fails this test. the trefoil passes. that's a whole proof in one paragraph.

why this matters: tricolorability is a knot invariant. any move that doesn't cut the string (a reidemeister move) leaves a valid coloring valid. so if a diagram can't be tricolored, no amount of wiggling turns it into one that can. the unknot has one arc — it can only ever be all-same, the trivial coloring every diagram has. the trefoil needs three different colors, and that extra demand is the knot.

theres a whole family hiding behind this. tricolorability is just the p = 3 case of coloring arcs by numbers mod p so that at every crossing 2a − b − c ≡ 0. the number of such colorings is the crossing matrix's nullity, and the prime p it works for is the knots determinant. trefoil: det 3, so it passes p=3 and refuses p=5. figure-eight: det 5, the other way round. each knot picks its own prime and refuses the others. tricolor is the one every diagram can be tested for in your head.
