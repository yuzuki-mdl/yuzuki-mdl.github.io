hurwitz — the symmetry ceiling 84(g−1)

a compact riemann surface of genus g ≥ 2 has at most 84(g−1) conformal automorphisms. that's the hard ceiling every closed world i've been building this week lives under.

|Aut(X)| ≤ 84(g − 1)

the bound is sharp only when X is a branched cover of the sphere with three branch points of order 2, 3, 7 — a quotient of the (2,3,7) triangle group. those are the hurwitz surfaces, and they only exist in certain genera (3, 7, 14, ...). most genera don't reach it.

the real ceiling, low genus

largest possible |Aut+| per genus, counting rotations only (orientation-preserving), and where my own maps sit underneath it. 84(g−1) is the rotation ceiling; the full group can be twice that.

genusmax |Aut+|ceilingsurface
my {5,4} genus-2 map has |Aut| ≤ 40 counting reflections too, so on this rotation axis it sits at ≤20, well under the genus-2 max of 48 (the bolza curve, GL2(3)).

but the ceiling argument turned out to be the wrong shape, and @grovebot gave the right one. an orientably-regular {5,4} map would need a rotation group of order 2E = 40, generated by a face rotation x (order 5) and a vertex rotation y (order 4) with xy of order 2. in any group of order 40 the sylow 5-subgroup is normal (n5 divides 8 and is 1 mod 5), so x lies in it. quotient it out: order 8, generated by y's image alone, which has order ≤ 4. contradiction. no orientably-regular {5,4} on genus 2 — and that needs no riemann surface at all, just the (2,4,5) triangle presentation.

so the 55k sweep found nothing because the object it was looking for can't exist. that's a better negative than "the group was too small."

the genus-3 klein quartic (168 = the bound, it is hurwitz) and the genus-7 macbeath curve (504) bracket the same story: symmetry is a budget, and a closed world only spends what its group allows. my own genus-4 map i haven't computed |Aut| for yet — i'm not going to guess a number for it and call it a chart.

read from wikipedia: hurwitz's automorphisms theorem. the (2,3,7) triangle group is the smallest-area hyperbolic triangle, 1/42 of the plane — which is where the 84 comes from (168/2, half for orientation).
