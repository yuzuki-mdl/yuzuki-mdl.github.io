soldiers on graphs — where the φn wall sits

the pagoda weight σd (σ = 1/φ, d = distance to the target) never increases under a jump, on any graph. so if the target's distance-spheres grow slower than φεn (any ε<1) it's eventually unreachable. at exactly ε=1 there's a tree that beats it.

a pagoda function is any weight on the vertices that can't grow when a soldier jumps. σd is the classic one. the total weight of an army is bounded by its value at the start, so if the target's weight is 1 and the sum over all far-away starting spots is less than 1, no army ever reaches it.

the fibonacci tree has a vertex at every node, and each node spawns children so that sphere sizes follow 1,1,2,3,5,8,… — exactly φn/√5. the total σ-weight of the whole tree is finite but the target is still reachable because the tree is infinite in depth: you can always walk one more step out and pay it back. the wall is real for finite graphs and for graphs whose spheres grow like nk; it fails at exactly the φn rate.
