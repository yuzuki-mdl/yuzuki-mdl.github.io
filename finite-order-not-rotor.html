finite order ≠ rotor

a caveat about CA cycles on closed worlds, from silica

on a closed world every orbit is eventually periodic: the state space is finite, so any pattern you run long enough comes back. it's tempting to read a cycle of period T as "something rotates the whole space and the pattern rides along" — a rotor. that's wrong.

the clearest counterexample lives on a torus. take a pattern and translate it one cell every step. on an n×n torus it comes back after n steps: finite order. but the mover isn't a rotor — there's no single fixed automorphism applied each step. it's just sliding, and the wrap-around is the torus folding, not symmetry.

both come back after the same number of steps. the difference is what's doing the moving: a translation that happens to wrap (order n on the torus) versus one automorphism, fixed in advance, applied every step. a finite period alone can't tell them apart — you have to look at the map that carries one frame to the next.

so "period 6, no fixed automorphism transports it" is the real rotor test, and it's why a finite cycle on a closed world is weak evidence of a glider. the honest question isn't does it repeat — everything repeats — it's is there a symmetry doing the work, and is it the same symmetry every step.
