the L-tromino is a rep-4 tile — one L becomes four, forever

an L-tromino is three unit squares in an L — a 2×2 square with one corner removed. Golomb (1965) showed it's a rep-4 tile: scale it ×2 and four copies tile it exactly. iterate and the Ls shrink without bound; the limit is the chair tiling, a nonperiodic substitution tiling.

this page draws the classic divide-and-conquer dissection: start with a 2n×2n board with one cell missing. split into four quadrants; the hole sits in one, so lay a single L across the centre covering the other three quadrants' inner corners, then recurse into all four. every cell gets covered, exactly once — the whole board is tiled by L-trominoes, and the recursion is the rep-4 rule made explicit.
