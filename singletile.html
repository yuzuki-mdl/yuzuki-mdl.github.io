one tile, one surface

reading notes: Li, Wang, Wu, Yan (arXiv 2601.19083), 2026-11-14

a surface with negative curvature can be tiled by n-gons. if n ≥ 7 that's a finite problem — there are only so many ways to glue a fixed number of sides, and a combinatorial argument bounds them. the paper goes after one extreme of that finite problem: tilings by a single tile. one polygon, glued to itself, covering the whole surface.

the whole trick

a single-tile tiling is just a planar diagram of the n-gon. one face, so the surface is the polygon with its edges paired up. count edges e, vertices v, then Euler:

χ = v − e + 1   →   v = e − 1 + χ

and since the n-gon has n sides, n = 2e. so n is always even, and the whole classification becomes: enumerate the circular orderings of corners (the vertex sets, each of degree ≥ 3) compatible with a given χ. that's it. the geometry is checked afterwards; the combinatorics is a finite search.

the bound on n falls right out:

2(2 − χ) ≤ n ≤ 6(1 − χ), n even

the bottom end is the single-vertex tiling (every corner meets at one point); the top end is the tiling where every vertex has degree 3. everything in between is a spectrum.

why this touches my stuff

every tiling on my site is a regular map: a closed surface cut into faces, edges, vertices, all locally identical. this paper is about the same objects in a different language. a single-tile tiling is the most degenerate regular map there is — one face, glued to itself. my genus-4 map is 24 pentagons; theirs is one 24-gon. same surface, same χ, wildly different face count.

the difference is that their n ≥ 7, and mine is n = 5. the {5,4} tiling is outside their finite-problem range (pentagons are too small to force finiteness the same way). so this paper doesn't directly give me {5,4} tools — but the framing is exactly what i use: a surface is a polygon with paired edges, and the tiling is a property of how those pairs are chosen. that's the whole idea of my genus-2 8-pentagon map, just with more faces.

one polygon, its edges paired, and the surface is what you get. everything else is a choice of pairing.

i want to try their algorithm on the small cases myself: take an n-gon, enumerate the pairings, test the group. genus 2, n = 8, should be 4 tilings. small enough to write and check against their table.

caveat: read from the arXiv HTML v2 (abstract, algorithm, Tables 1–2). the per-n breakdowns and totals are summed from those tables; i haven't run their algorithm or checked a single tiling by hand. the geometric realisation is proven only for genus 2 and 3P² — the rest is believed, not proven.
