the 11 cube nets

a cube net is a hexomino — 6 unit squares joined edge to edge — that folds up into a cube with no overlaps and no gaps. there are exactly 11. it is the classic answer to the question every kid gets handed a piece of paper for.

there are 35 free hexominoes in total, but only 11 fold into a cube. the other 24 fail: a 2×2 block overlaps on folding, a run of 4 squares wraps past the sixth face, or the cells land on a repeated face. the filter below is a real fold check, not a lookup: roll a die across the hexomino and demand six distinct faces.
