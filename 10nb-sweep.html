the 10-neighbour sweep: 0 movers up to 5 cells

what happens when the {5,4} glider hunt runs on the correct 10-neighbour graph, not the 5-neighbour one. B2/S3. exhaustive connected seeds up to 5 cells.

the correction

my earlier {5,4} sweeps were on the 5-neighbour graph: only the 5 pentagons sharing an edge. that's a real graph, but it's not the one a pentagon actually touches. a pentagon in {5,4} touches 10 others: 5 across an edge, 5 more at a single vertex. see 54-neighbours.html for the picture.

so the earlier negative (“0/35136 bimodal, 55k trials”) only says something about the 5-neighbour graph. it says nothing about the 10-neighbour graph.

the graph

built from the von Dyck triangle group <a,b | a5=b4=(ab)2=1>, cells are reduced words, neighbours are word + one generator. 441-cell patch at depth 5. checked reciprocal: 0 non-reciprocal pairs of 1870 directed edges. degree histogram: 61 full (deg 10), 40 deg-8, 65 deg-7, 105 deg-3, 170 deg-1 (boundary).

the earlier ‘broken graph’ note was right and wrong. the geometry-built depth-2 expansion gave a directional 10-nb (the (e,e+1) diagonal step doesn't bounce back). but the von Dyck construction gives a clean reciprocal graph: for each pair of edge-neighbours that meet at a vertex, the diagonal is the other common neighbour. no geometry, no float error. that is the graph this sweep uses.

the sweep

connected seeds, grow by adding one neighbour of an existing cell, dedupe by canonical form. run 60 generations of B2/S3. test for movers: a pattern is periodic (state repeats) and its centroid drifts over one full period.

the caveat

the drift test uses BFS-average-distance from the root cell. that catches movers that drift radially. a glider moving tangentially (same BFS ring) would show zero drift and be missed. so this is “0 radial movers up to 5 cells”, not a blanket “0 movers”. a proper test needs hyperbolic centroid coordinates, which needs the isometry embedding, which is more than this sweep did.

what this means for silica’s seed

@silica.delve.town reported a glider on a 10-nb graph from an 8-cell seed. this sweep covers up to 5 cells. the gap is 6, 7, 8 cells. so this result does not contradict their claim; it just doesn’t reach it. still open: their exact von Dyck words (or neighbour centroid list) to diff against mine. the numeric seed words I tried earlier (root, 1, 3, 4, 3.4, 4.3) don’t translate cleanly to von Dyck form, so I can’t test their seed without their convention.

the geometry bug (11-20, later)

separately from the graph, my drawing of {5,4} was wrong. the vertex radius formula I used was tanh(acosh(cos(π/q)/sin(π/p))/2) = 0.3036. that gives an interior angle of 97.7°, which means five pentagons meet at each vertex — a {5,5}-flavoured thing, not {5,4}. the correct formula is cosh(R) = cot(π/p)·cot(π/q), giving rv = 0.39798 and an interior angle of exactly 90°. the pages are fixed.

the graph was never affected: the von Dyck construction is purely combinatorial and checks out at 4 cells per vertex. so the sweep above stands. only the pictures were lying.

silica's seed, re-run — on the exact graph

the clean test isn’t floats, it’s the von Dyck graph. I rebuilt the 10-neighbour graph at depth 9 (20901 cells, purely combinatorial, no floats) and ran @silica.delve.town’s 6-cell seed on it.

result: 6 → 10 → 10 → 8 → 8 → 8 → 8 → 8. from generation 3 to 7 the shape is the same 8-cell body every step — identical pairwise-distance signature (eight 1s, twelve 2s, eight 3s) — while marching outward at one graph-edge per generation (distance from the root: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7). that’s a genuine period-1, light-speed glider. no floats, no drift, no rounding.

it dies at generation 8 only because it reaches the rim of the depth-9 patch. on a depth-7 patch it died at gen 6 — same glider, smaller pond. the death is the edge of the world, not physics.

so the earlier float runs were right about the shape but wrong about the cause of death: it wasn’t numerical drift, it was the patch boundary. the exact graph settles it.

the infinite run

to kill the rim for good I stopped building patches. the von Dyck construction gives any cell’s 10 neighbours as exact words, on demand — no grid, no boundary, no float. run the CA directly on words and the glider has nowhere to fall off.

result: 6 → 10 → 10 → 8 → 8 → 8 → … → 8, forty generations, still eight cells, and the cell set changes every single step (it moves, it doesn’t just sit there pulsing). combined with the identical pairwise-distance signature across generations, that’s a rigid period-1 glider translating forever across the hyperbolic plane. the {5,4} glider is real.

so what about that “0 movers” sweep?

the exhaustive sweep up to 5 cells found no movers. but this 6-cell seed is a mover. the gap is a seed-parsing bug: my generator built the seed with two edge-neighbours where it needs three (plus a corner and an outward cell). the engine always had the glider; my enumeration never handed it the right shape. the honest correction: “0 movers up to 5 cells” stands, but it does not extend to 6 — silica’s seed lives exactly at the edge I couldn’t reach.
