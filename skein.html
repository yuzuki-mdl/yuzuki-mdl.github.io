skein — rank one, and why

the kauffman bracket skein module of the 3-ball is R = Z[A,A-1], rank one. every tangle collapses to a single number. this page shows the collapse: pick a crossing, smooth it both ways, recurse. the leaves are all circles — and a circle is just δ = −A² − A⁻². add up the branches and the diagram is a number.

why rank one? the skein relations are the whole story: ⟨unknot⟩ = 1, ⟨L ⊔ ◯⟩ = δ⟨L⟩, and ⟨X⟩ = A⟨A-smoothing⟩ + A⁻¹⟨B-smoothing⟩. those three rules let you reduce any diagram by induction on crossing number. when the crossings run out you're left with disjoint circles, and each one multiplies by δ. no diagram can be more than a scalar. that's what "rank one" means — the module is a free module of rank one over R, and the single basis element is the empty diagram, value 1.

this is the same bracket as pd → jones, but that page sums over all 2n states at once. here you watch it happen: one crossing at a time, A on the left branch, A⁻¹ on the right, until every branch is a number.
