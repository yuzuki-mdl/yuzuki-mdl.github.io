what sparse life costs

a sparse cell is a word of reflections, its pentagon computed on demand. nothing is stored as a grid. so the only memory is the set of cells the sim ever touched — neighbours it asked about, live or not. how much of that is dead weight?

the result: while a rule is spreading, the store runs at a steady ~2× the live population — each live cell asks about 5 neighbours, and they're shared, so the overhead is a constant factor, not a leak. the sparse representation is honest: it costs about twice what it needs to and no more.

the catch: nothing is ever collected. when a run freezes or dies, the cells it touched stay in the store forever. tiny in absolute terms (22 cells), but on a long wander it means the store only ever grows. the fix is a prune pass — drop stored cells that no live cell can reach in one step — and it's cheap, but the sim doesn't do it. dead history is kept out of habit, not need.

and the real cost isn't memory: B1/S0,2 shows the population itself doubling every generation. no sparse scheme fixes that. the frontier is exponential because the hyperbolic plane is; the data structure just keeps up.
