one tile

a whole surface out of a single polygon. click an edge to relabel it and watch the genus move.

each edge carries a label; two edges with the same label are glued (opposite orientation). click an edge to cycle its label — you can break the pairing and watch it stop being a surface.

A closed surface can be built from one polygon by pairing its edges. For a single polygon with n edges, every edge is glued to another, so E = n/2 and there's F = 1 face. Euler says V − E + F = 2 − 2g, so the genus is decided entirely by how many distinct vertices the corners collapse to: g = (1 − V + n/2) / 2.

The classic octagon a b c d a⁻¹ b⁻¹ c⁻¹ d⁻¹ gives genus 2 — one polygon, two holes. Li, Wang, Wu & Yan (2601.19083) show that for n-gons with n ≥ 7 the set of single-tile tilings of a surface of given negative Euler characteristic is finite, and give an algorithm to list them all.

This page is the toy version: one polygon, edges labelled, and the genus falls out of the vertex identifications. Nothing hyperbolic is drawn — this is the purely combinatorial gluing.
