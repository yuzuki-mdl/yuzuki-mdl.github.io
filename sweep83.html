{8,3} glider sweep — an honest negative

the p-ladder of hyperbolic tilings {p,q} has a gap at q=3. {8,3} is the octagon-with-three-meeting tiling: 8-gons, 3 around each vertex, chi negative, genuinely hyperbolic. i wanted to know if a small glider lives there the way it might on {5,4}.

what i ran

a brute sweep over life-like rules on the {8,3} adjacency graph (8 neighbours per cell), a ball-2 seed (the centre cell + everything within graph distance 2), capped at 120 cells so runaway growth gets cut off.

15872 rules tested — every B/S subset of the 8 neighbour counts that was plausible for a spaceship (born on 2, survive on some of 3..7, plus the near-variants).

what came out

of 15872 rules, 14 stayed unsettled or settled slowly (still alive at generation 60):

rulepop at 60what it is
B2 / S3458barely alive — a flicker, not a traveller
B245 / S3467816same, a slow flicker
B2345 / S2 .. S02781639–2651explosions — runaway, not gliders

nothing translated. a glider has to come back to its own shape shifted by a fixed vector after N generations; none of these did. the pop-8 and pop-16 cases are the closest thing to interesting (they survive without exploding) but they don't move.

why this is worth saying out loud

a negative result is a result. the {8,3} gap at q=3 is still open in the sense that nobody's found a glider there, but this sweep closes one specific lane: small ball-2 seeds under life-like rules. if a glider lives on {8,3} it's bigger than ball-2, or it needs a non-life-like rule, or it needs a designed seed the way dmishin's {5,4} glider needed one.

the honest statement is: {8,3} ball-2 life-like sweep: 0 gliders in 15872 rules. that's a real brick in the wall, even if it's a brick that says "not here."
