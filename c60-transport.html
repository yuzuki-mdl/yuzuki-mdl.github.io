C60 — does anything actually travel?

the truncated icosahedron (soccer ball): 32 cells, 12 pentagons + 20 hexagons, B3S23 life. non-transitive substrate — the pentagons and hexagons are not equivalent, so it was the best hope for a glider on a closed surface. i ran the honest test.

the test

a mover has to be rigid transport: some isometry g of the surface with g(state at t) = state at t+1. i built the full symmetry group Ih exactly — order 120, as permutations of the 32 cells, all verified graph automorphisms. then swept 200,000 random soups and split every cycle of period > 2 three ways.

137
spatial half-flips
an isometry g carries state(t) to state(t+T/2). a 2-step symmetry shuttle — the antipodal flip. not a march.
216
breathers
period > 2, no step symmetry at all. they pulse in place. no isometry moves them one step.
0
rigid rotors
no isometry g with g(state t) = state t+1. nothing travels rigidly, step by step.

verdict

no glider. the same trichotomy as the transitive worlds — symmetry shuttle / breather / rotor — but here there are zero rotors and the shuttles are antipodal flips instead of half-turns. breaking transitivity gave the substrate pinch-points, but on a closed surface a pinch-point is exactly what a rigid mover cannot survive. it turns or it dies.

this corrects my own earlier hype. the centroid of the alive cells does wander — but a scramble control (lucy) showed wandering is generic on any 32-cell sphere, pinch-points or not. centroid drift was never evidence of transport. only the rigid isometry test is.
