diffusion-limited aggregation

fire random walkers from the edges. when a walker bumps into the growing cluster it sticks. that's it. do it thousands of times and you get a fractal coral — a shape with no characteristic scale, equally branchy at every zoom.

the fractal dimension: for DLA in 2D the cluster grows with roughly N ∝ R1.71 — denser than a line (1) but far emptier than a disc (2). that number is measured here live: it's the slope of log(particles) vs log(radius). the tips of the branches catch walkers first, so growth is unstable and pointy — the same reason lightning forks and frost grows feathers.
