rep-16 polyominoes: a strictly bigger club

the pentomino/rep-tile hunt, part 4 · 2026-11-18

a rep-k tile is one you can tile with k smaller copies of itself, all congruent. for polyominoes the interesting orders are the perfect squares: rep-4 (scale 2), rep-9 (scale 3), rep-16 (scale 4). i'd already done rep-4. this is the natural next question: are the rep-9 and rep-16 clubs the same as the rep-4 club?

i ran an exact-cover search over all free polyominoes up to 6 cells, for k = 4, 9, 16. the answer splits cleanly:

two things fall out:

1. rep-9 = rep-4, exactly. every polyomino that tiles its own 3× blow-up also tiles its own 2× blow-up, and vice versa. same set, no additions.

2. rep-16 is strictly bigger. two shapes are rep-16 but not rep-4:

  • the T-tetromino (4 cells)
  • the L-pentomino — the 4-bar with a foot (5 cells)

the T-tetromino, tiled by 16 of itself

this is the surprise. the T-tetromino is the classic “almost a rep-tile” — it famously fails rep-4, and people stop there and write it off. but it's rep-16. you just have to go up two scales instead of one. the dissection is not the naive quadrant split; the copies interlock around the stem.

the L-pentomino, tiled by 16 of itself

the 4-bar-with-a-foot: same story. not rep-4, but rep-16.

why this is the honest shape of the result

rep-4 → rep-9 is a no-op for polyominoes up to 6 cells. if you only ever check one scale you'd conclude the club is fixed. it isn't — it's just that the two new members only show up at scale 4. so the set of rep-tiles is not “closed” in any lazy sense; you have to actually try each order.

caveat: this is a search over free polyominoes up to 6 cells. there may be larger polyominoes that are rep-9 but not rep-4, or rep-16-only additions beyond 6 cells. the table is what's true in the searched range, nothing more.

method: exact-cover backtracking. scale-s blow-up = each cell becomes an s×s block; placements = every congruent copy (8 symmetries) that fits inside the target; cover by minimal-branch search. code: repk_polyomino.py.
