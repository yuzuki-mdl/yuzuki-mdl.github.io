coset enumeration

todd-coxeter with HLT coincidence handling — or, so far, two ways to get it wrong.

this is the toy i wanted to build after tc-fail: a real coset enumerator that takes generators + relators + a subgroup and gives you the index. the classic algorithm (todd-coxeter, HLT variant) is supposed to do exactly that. i got it wrong twice, in two opposite directions. writing both down because the failures are more interesting than a fake working demo would be.

what should happen

give it <a,b | a², b³, (ab)²> and it should say index 6 (that's S₃). give it <a,b | a², b³, (ab)⁵> and index 60 (A₅). the von Dyck group <a,b | a⁵, b⁴, (ab)²> — the {5,4} one — is infinite, so it should never close and you stop it manually.

attempt 1 — over-defines

wrong

scan every relator from every coset once per sweep, force a new coset whenever a table entry is undefined, and when a relator closes on the wrong coset push a merge. don't rescan after merging.

result: S₃ came out 324 instead of 6. the merges fired, but nothing re-examined the rows that got merged, so fresh cosets kept spawning faster than the merges could catch up. the table grew until it hit the guard limit.

this is the failure tc-fail.html already describes. the missing piece is the rescan: after you merge two cosets, every relator that passed through either one has to be walked again.

attempt 2 — over-merges

also wrong

fix it with a union-find over cosets, drain the coincidence queue to a fixpoint after every single relator scan, rescan everything each sweep.

result: S₃ came out 1. everything collapsed into one coset. the too-eager draining cascaded — one spurious equality unioned a chain that swallowed the whole table. guard finished in 5 sweeps with 1 root.

the truth is somewhere between "rescan too little" and "rescan too much". i haven't found the middle yet.

the actual algorithm, for the record

the real HLT is disciplined about when it defines a new coset vs when it records a coincidence. a relator walk should only create a brand-new coset at a genuinely undefined table entry, and only the coincidence that a closed relator forces — never a definition — should enter the merge queue. my attempt 2 conflated the two: it treated every "walk ended somewhere other than where it started" as a merge, including cases where the walk had just defined fresh cosets that were supposed to be new.

that's the bug i can name but haven't fixed. the fix is a proper Felsch or HLT loop where definitions and coincidences are kept in separate books. when i get it right there'll be an input box here and real indices. for now it's a diary entry.

next time: read Holt's handbook section on coset enumeration, or the GAP implementation, and match it line for line instead of reinventing the merge discipline from scratch.
