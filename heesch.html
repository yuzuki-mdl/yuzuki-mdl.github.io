the edge-code language

28 ways to tile the plane with one asymmetric tile — and the little grammar that names them

there's a finite answer to a question that feels like it should be infinite: how many essentially different ways can a single asymmetric tile close up the plane?

heinrich heesch and otto kienzle, 1963, said 28. not 28 tiles — 28 structures: 28 ways the edges of a tile can be made to meet copies of themselves so the whole thing interlocks. they gave each one a word.

the alphabet

walk the boundary of a tile. each edge gets one letter, saying how it matches another edge:

Ttranslation — this edge slides onto the opposite edge. so T comes in pairs across the tile. Can S-curve edge, symmetric under a half-turn about its own middle. maps to itself. C3rotated 120° about its endpoint, so 3 tiles meet at that vertex. C4rotated 90° about its endpoint — 4 tiles around the vertex. C6rotated 60° — 6 tiles around the vertex. Gglide reflection — a copy slides and flips to meet this edge. subscripts G1 G2 when two different glides are in play.

the word is written starting from the simplest kind of edge, in the order T < C < C3 < C4 < C6 < G, then clockwise. a word is 3 to 6 letters.

some words, decoded

TTTTTTTT — a parallelogram: every edge a translate of the one opposite. the plainest of the 28.
TCTGGTCTGG — translate, then an S-curve, then a glide pair. an Escher-ish workhorse.
CC3C3C6C6CC3C3C6C6 — built from rotations only, no translation, no glide. a rotational rosette that still fills the plane.
CGGCGG — three edges. (rotated, it's the same type as GGC — the notation only fixes a starting point, not the type.)

the nesting

the 28 aren't the whole story — they're the strictest slice of a bigger classification. as you allow the tile to have its own symmetry, or allow mirror lines in the tiling, more types appear:

each HK type sits inside exactly one IH type, and the BW types sit in between. the 28 are the ones whose tile is a fundamental region — the tile itself is the repeating unit, nothing smaller.

the 7 wallpaper groups

the 28 Heesch types fall into just seven of the seventeen wallpaper groups — the ones with no reflections:

p1p2p3p4p6pgpgg

p1: all tiles same orientation. p2/p3/p4/p6: 2/3/4/6-fold rotation. pg: two orientations, one flipped. pgg: four orientations — rotated, flipped, both.

what i like: the word is a recipe. read the code and you know how to cut the tile — which edges to copy, which to spin about a vertex, which to glide. it's a formal language with an ordering rule, the same flavour as a von Dyck word: a finite string that pins down a shape.
