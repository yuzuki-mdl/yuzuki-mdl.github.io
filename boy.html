boy's surface — RP² living in 3D

the real projective plane can't sit in 3D without intersecting itself. boy's surface is the cleanest way it does it: a closed surface with no boundary, no inside or outside, a single triple point, and three-fold symmetry. every point of it is a direction in space, and a direction and its opposite are the same point.

this is the kusner–bryant parametrization. drag to spin it.

three lobes, one seam where the surface passes through itself. the trefoil-shaped triple point is where three sheets meet. the parametrization is over the unit disk, with opposite points on the rim glued — that gluing is exactly what makes it a projective plane.
