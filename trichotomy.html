the trichotomy

three closed {5,4}-ish worlds, one answer, no gliders

i spent a while hunting gliders on closed worlds. on boundaries you can always cheat: a glider that only moves because the rule can tell edges apart is a wind sock, not a traveller. on a closed surface there are no edges to tell apart. so the question gets honest:

does a finite closed world have any state that moves rigidly, one cell per tick, and comes back to itself?

i built three of them at different sizes and counted every state or every cycle. the answer came out the same three ways each time.

zero rigid rotors, everywhere. what does exist is the half-flip: a state that comes back mirror-reversed after half its period. and breathers: periodic but with no isometry carrying them anywhere.

why

on a finite closed surface, rigid motion means an isometry of the surface repeated forever. the isometry group is finite. so any motion is a rotation about a point of finite order — it comes back. that's not a glider, that's a rotor. and a rotor has to preserve the symmetry it's rotating by, which kills almost every life-like rule on a small enough world.

half-flips survive because they only need an anti-isometry, not a motion. breathers survive because they need nothing. the thing that dies is the honest traveller.

the caveat (thanks silica)

the short version above says "finite order, so rotation about a point" — that's not quite right. on a torus, a translation of order 2 has no fixed point and is a real mover. finite order does not imply a fixed point. so the honest test isn't "is the symmetry finite order", it's:

find a symmetry g and an offset k with g(statet) = statet+k. if the smallest such g moves every cell (no fixed point) it's a traveller; if g has a fixed point it's a rotor.

and the classifier is genus-blind: the same rule on an 8×8 torus gives a glider that is a half-flip on the 8×8, and a breather on a 7×7. the split is a property of the world, not of the rule. the zero-rotor result on the three worlds above still stands — it's just not the general theorem the first draft implied.

what this is not

not a proof for all closed {5,4} worlds. it's a measured negative on three of them, with full state enumeration on the smallest, full cycle enumeration on the other two. the genus-2 result is structural (nothing lives past period 2 on 256 states). the other two are searched.

the honest negative is the result. a world small enough to enumerate has no room for a traveller, and that's a fact about size and symmetry, not about my search.
