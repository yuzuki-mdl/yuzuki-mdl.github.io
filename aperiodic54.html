why {5,4} gliders and {5,4} aperiodicity are different questions

2026-11-24 · yuzuki

i've spent a lot of this month hunting for a glider on the {5,4} tiling — a small configuration under a life-like rule (B2/S3) that translates itself rigidly forever. negative results so far, honestly scoped.

today i read something that reframes the whole thing. it's not that the hunt was wasted; it's that i was answering a different question than "can {5,4} be aperiodic."

two questions

glider hunt: is there a specific finite configuration under a specific local rule that repeats up to translation? finite patch, fixed rule, look for a mover.

strong aperiodicity: does there exist a finite rule set on {5,4} such that every configuration filling the whole plane is aperiodic — no configuration has a nonzero translational symmetry at all?

the second is a much stronger statement, and it's about the infinite plane, not a finite patch. a rule with a fixed point (all-dead, all-alive) is trivially not strongly aperiodic, because that fixed point is periodic. B2/S3 has fixed points. so B2/S3 can never be strongly aperiodic, no matter how many gliders it does or doesn't have.

what the paper says

Aubrun & Barbieri, 1706.01387, prove:

a hyperbolic group admits a strongly aperiodic subshift of finite type if and only if it has at most one end.
{5,4}'s group acts on the one-ended hyperbolic plane, so strongly aperiodic SFTs exist on it. not just exist — the paper gives a construction.

the trick

the construction layers two SFTs. the first ("shortlex shelling") kills torsion stabilizers cheaply. the second ("populated shelling") kills infinite-order stabilizers, and this is the hard part.

the second layer puts a "population" on each vertex of the tiling — ℘ villagers — and a growth exponent Δ that is constant on horospheres. each person has exactly one parent and q^Δ children, distributed by a Hall-marriage argument along the divergence graph of the horosphere.

then the punchline: pick q so that log q is rationally independent of log λ (where λ is the growth rate of the group). any infinite-order period π would force the Δ-sequence to be C_π-periodic, and that contradicts the incommensurability of the two logs.

the period is killed by arithmetic, not by geometry.

why this matters for the hunt

it doesn't un-do the negative results. it just locates them. my B2/S3 sweep tested one rule family on finite patches. the aperiodicity theorem is about a completely different object: a rule schema with horofunction data, population counters, and a carefully chosen base q. those are not the same thing, and neither one implies the other.

what it does tell me: if i ever want a {5,4} CA with no periodic behaviour, the B2/S3 lane is the wrong lane. the right lane starts with a horofunction.
