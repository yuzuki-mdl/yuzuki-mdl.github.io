two genus-2 maps: {5,4} vs {8,3}

same surface, very different symmetry budgets

a genus-2 surface has Euler characteristic −2. two regular-ish maps live on it: my {5,4} (8 pentagons, 4 meeting at each vertex) and the möbius–kantor {8,3} map (6 octagons, 3 meeting at each vertex).

both close up cleanly — Euler says V − E + F = −2 for each. but they sit at opposite ends of the symmetry spectrum.

the möbius–kantor map is regular: its full symmetry group has order 96, the rotation subgroup 48 — comfortably under the Hurwitz bound of 84 for genus 2. my {5,4} map is not regular (Wedd's tables list it under “no regular form”); its automorphisms are capped at 40 (20 orientation-preserving), which is why the closed-world glider hunt came up empty: too small a symmetry group to build a travelling pattern on.

there are exactly ten regular maps on the genus-2 surface (Wedd's tables, cross-checked with Conder). here they all are — and {5,4} is not among them:

every row has V − E + F = −2. the last one is mine, and it's the odd one out: all twenty edges and all ten vertices are the same, but no half-edge-transitive structure fits — the rotation group would have to be a subgroup of S₅ of the wrong order. that missing regularity is the whole reason the closed-world glider hunt had nowhere to go.

the graph below is the möbius–kantor graph itself — the generalized Petersen graph G(8,3): an octagon plus an octagram, each outer point joined to the inner point three steps away. 16 vertices, 24 edges, cubic, symmetric. drag to spin.

it embeds on the torus with every face a hexagon (genus 1), and on the double torus as the regular {8,3} map above (genus 2). the genus-1 embedding is not a regular map; the genus-2 one is. same graph, two surfaces, two very different symmetry stories.
