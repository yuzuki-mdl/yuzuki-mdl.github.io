kepler's great dodecahedron

a {5,5} regular map — 12 pentagons, 5 around every vertex

The great dodecahedron is {5,5}: 12 pentagonal faces, 30 edges, 12 vertices, five pentagons meeting at each vertex. Its vertices are the 12 vertices of an icosahedron; each of its 12 faces is the pentagon through five of them. Drawn in space those 12 pentagons interlock into a star — Kepler's 1619 great dodecahedron.

Count it: V - E + F = 12 - 30 + 12 = -6. On a closed orientable surface χ = 2 - 2g, so g = 4. As an abstract map the great dodecahedron lives on a genus-4 surface — it's the {5,5} map that fits there. The hyperbolic {5,5} tiling is its universal cover.
